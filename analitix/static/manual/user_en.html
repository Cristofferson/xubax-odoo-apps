1. The idea, in one paragraph

Your point of sale tells you what you sold. Analitix tells you who you did not sell to, and why. It counts how many people came in, crosses that against your tickets, and gives you the figure no ERP holds: your conversion rate. Everything else is built on top — where people stop, how long they wait, who was never spoken to, and what that cost you.

This is not a people counter. The counter is the foundation. "You had 400 visitors" helps nobody. "Eleven people waited at the ring counter and nine were never spoken to" does.

2. Setting up your store

Everything that differs between stores is configuration. Nothing is baked into the code: not the number of doors, not the thresholds, not who gets the alerts.

  1. Quick setup. Go to Analitix → Configuration → New Store Setup. The wizard creates the store, its doors and each door's devices in one step.
  2. Doors. Add as many as the store actually has: one, three, seven. On a staff or service door, clear "Counts visitors" — it stays measured, but its traffic stops distorting the conversion rate.
  3. How sales are attributed. On the "Sales Matching" tab you choose between specific registers (when several stores share one company) and the whole company (when the company runs a single store). Getting this wrong is the number one cause of a conversion rate that does not add up.
  4. Square metres. Optional. With them you get the density figures: revenue per m² and visitors per m².
  5. Staff. Under "Staff Exclusion" you enrol each employee's face signature so their crossings do not inflate the visitor count. Without it, a salesperson who steps outside ten times a day shows up as ten customers.

The kill switch. The store form carries a Pause capture button. It stops data ingest immediately, without anyone having to reach a piece of hardware. It is there for the day you need it and do not want to go looking for someone.

4. Zones and displays

A zone is a named part of the floor: "Engagement Rings", "Fitting Rooms", "Checkout". A display is a case or table inside a zone.

With those, "twelve minutes inside" becomes "eleven of those twelve at the ring counter, and nobody went over". Each zone carries its own thresholds, because a fitting room and a till are not the same thing: lingering in a fitting room is the point; lingering at a till is being ignored.

On each display you can record what is physically on it. Only you know, and without it the attention figures cannot be compared against sales — which is where the finding lives: draws a crowd and sells little (price, or nobody closing) versus sells well from a cold corner (move it to the window).

5. Lost-sale alerts

An alert fires when all three happen together: somebody lingers past the zone's threshold, nobody served them, and there was no ticket. Each one alone is completely ordinary; the three together are a sale that walked out.

How the alert arrives

You pick the channels, and you can have several at once. Three are discreet — only the assigned salesperson perceives them:

  • Odoo app: a push on their phone plus an activity, so it survives a locked screen.
  • Odoo chat (Discuss): a direct message. On a floor where the team keeps Discuss open all day, this is often the fastest channel.
  • WhatsApp: for staff who do not keep the Odoo app open. Requires Odoo's WhatsApp module.

Two are not discreet and ship switched off:

  • Audible chime: if the phone is not on silent, or the alert lands on a back-office machine, the customer may hear it and understand they are being discussed.
  • Signage screen: whatever appears there is read by the customer standing in front of it. Right for a message written for them, wrong for one about them.

Who receives it

The salesperson covering that zone at that moment, from the per-zone, per-weekday, per-hour rota. Whoever covers the ring counter at 11:00 is not whoever covers it at 19:00. If nobody is on duty, the alert escalates to the store's fallback user rather than evaporating.

A lot of escalations to the fallback does not mean the system is broken: it means the shift map is wrong.

What to do when one arrives

Go over, and acknowledge the alert from your phone. That acknowledgement is what feeds the floor coaching report: how many alerts each person received, how long they took to respond, and how many of the ones they did answer ended in a sale. It is measured against what they answered, not what they were sent, because nobody should be marked down for a nudge that arrived while they were with another customer.

6. The personal welcome

If the store has customer identification switched on, when somebody you already know walks in, the salesperson gets their name, their last purchase and whatever context exists in CRM or in special dates.

The social rule. A screen that says "Welcome back, Gustavo" while Gustavo is standing there with somebody has just told that person something about him. So when he is not alone, the screen stays neutral and the personal greeting goes to the salesperson instead. You can switch this rule off, but it is on by default and for a reason.

7. Screens and signage

If you have screens connected, signage rules let measured events change what they show. Each rule has a trigger (walk-out, group, profile, quiet hour, welcome), conditions you define, and a target.

The target resolves where before what: a bundle offer belongs on the screen the group is standing near, not on the one by the door. Every rule also carries a minimum gap between firings, because without it a busy hour turns your signage into a flicker.

8. Staff attendance

Off by default. If you switch it on, employee crossings at the staff door become check-ins and check-outs in Odoo's native attendance (hr.attendance) — the one payroll, leave and all your existing reports already read.

  • It never overwrites a manual correction. If someone fixed a check-in by hand, the camera does not argue.
  • Stepping out does not end the day. Repeat crossings inside a configurable gap are ignored.
  • Forgotten shifts are closed overnight at the store's maximum shift length, and the record says it was a guess rather than an observation. Correct it if the real time matters.

This records your team's working hours. A store that has not agreed it with its people should not switch it on because a camera was installed. The decision belongs to the employer, and it is deliberate.

9. The watch list

This is the only feature in Analitix that names a specific person, and it is built with more friction than any other on purpose.

  • Nobody is added automatically. Ever. A person writes the entry, with a reason and a date.
  • It takes two people. A new entry recognises nobody until a second authorised person confirms it, and the person who added it cannot be the one who confirms.
  • It expires. Every entry carries a review date and an expiry. Letting it lapse is the normal outcome; extending it is a decision, and it is recorded.
  • A match is a prompt to pay attention, never an accusation and never an automatic action. It appears on no screen and never reaches the sales floor at large. It goes quietly to a small group you name yourself.
  • Record the mistakes. When a match was the wrong person, mark it. A list whose false positives nobody writes down is a list nobody can fix.

Only the Security / Compliance role can see it, and that role is deliberately not given to the store manager by default: somebody being on a watch list is not commercial information. Who reads the list is logged too, not only who changes it.

10. The monthly report

Each month the owner receives a plain-language e-mail: visitors, conversion, walk-outs spotted, how many the team managed to rescue, and roughly what that was worth.

The recovered figure is an estimate and is labelled as one everywhere it appears. It is "rescued walk-outs × your own average sale". A rescued walk-out is one where an alert fired and that same visit later bought something: correlation, honestly stated, not proof. Inflating that number is the fastest way to lose the customer who eventually checks it.

11. Privacy: what is stored and what is not

NeverYes, and for how long
Images. Video. Photographs. None of it leaves the camera or reaches Odoo. Irreversible numeric vectors ("signatures"), encrypted in the database.
Names of anonymous customers. Anonymous signatures under an opaque handle, deleted automatically at the retention window you set (3 hours by default).
A biometric customer index acquired by accident. Identified signatures only if you switch customer identification on and the customer handed over their details at the till. Off by default, with its own expiry, and every read audited.

Recognition runs on the machine in your store (the "edge"). Odoo receives numbers and counts, never pixels.

SymptomMost common cause

Too many visitorsMissing staff signatures, or the virtual line sits across a waiting area rather than in the doorway.
"Hour with sales and no visitors" appearsA counter went down. Check Operations → Device Health.

The watch list never matches anybodyAlmost always liveness: cameras that do not report it send zero, and the list refuses to compare. Covered in the implementation guide.
